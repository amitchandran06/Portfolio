01The Brief

This project comes as part of the UCL Rover Team, which I am part of. We are making strides to compete in the European Rover Challenge (ERC). In addition to building a rover, it must also be accompanied with a custom-built drone. The drone must be capable of navigating around a dynamic space including landing and taking off autonomously, which is the focus of my role. This is an active project, still under development.

02Design Philosophy

Much of the design for this project is strictly dictated by the regulations for the competition. The general philosophy followed that of 'edge computing'. We had to process as much information onboard the drone, sending only basic telemetry back to a central computer. However, due to the ongoing nature of the project, the design is still open to alterations.

03Technical Depth & Achievements

a
Real-time Computer VisionThe drone had to be capable of detecting Aruco markers on the ground, to serve as landing references. By calibrating the camera, we were able to accurately detect the relative to pose to markers within 1cm.
b
Networking & ControlAfter processing the pose of the Aruco markers, this data is then transmitted to Simulink via UDP. Simulink then makes use of MAVLink to package and transmit to the flight controller. This completes the connection between a top level object detection algorithm, and the low level motor control of the FC .
